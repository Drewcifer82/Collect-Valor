COLLECT VALOR

30TH CELEBRATION · MARKET WATCH

The 30th Celebration market is finding its level.

The early hype was huge. A few days later, the market is starting to tell us what these cards are actually worth.

In my last post, I said I was not in a hurry to buy into Pokémon's 30th Celebration set. I wanted to let people open packs, let more product reach collectors, and give the market time to settle. That is exactly what is happening now.

Before release, nobody really knew the pull rates. The big cards were getting talked about everywhere, early listings were high, and it was easy to feel like you had to buy immediately or miss out. But collectors have now opened a lot of product, and the supply picture looks much different than it did during the hype.

Five cards the market was watching

These TCGplayer market prices are a current snapshot, not a promise of where any card will go next:

Those are still valuable cards. Crystal Lugia remains a major chase, and Mew, Pikachu, and Mewtwo are characters collectors genuinely care about. But compared with the wild numbers being discussed before release, the market has come back down to earth fast.

Why that can be good for collectors

This is definitely a good thing when it comes to the dreaded scalpers. A lot of people bought product because they expected the cards inside to stay extremely expensive. They were betting on low pull rates, limited supply, and collectors feeling pressured to pay whatever price was being asked.

But when people started opening product and pulling multiple big cards, the supply picture changed quickly. I watched live openings where the big hits showed up far more often than people expected. That does not mean every box is loaded, but it helps explain why the early prices are correcting.

The pull rate appears high enough to diminish single-card prices. For collectors, that can be a win. It means more people can chase cards they actually want without paying pre-release hype prices. It also reminds us why patience matters: a card is not automatically worth thousands of dollars just because it is new, rare-looking, and surrounded by excitement.

Nobody can promise where prices go. This is not financial advice—just a collector's reminder to separate early hype from real market activity.

I am still waiting on boxes

I am personally not in any hurry to go buy boxes—not until we get a lot closer to MSRP.

The first wave has been expensive, and a lot of that price came from fear of missing out. If pull rates stay generous and more product arrives in later waves, I think sealed prices could cool down too. That would be great for collectors who want to open packs, build the Pikachu sub-series, chase the Classic Collection, or simply enjoy the 30th anniversary without treating every release like a stock market event.

For now, I am watching, learning, and waiting. The set is still brand new. We do not need to rush.
